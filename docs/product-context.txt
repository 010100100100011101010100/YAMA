1. PRODUCT MISSION
Project Yama is an AI-assisted incident intelligence platform.
Its purpose is to help people who have experienced or witnessed crimes,
fraud, abuse, harassment, or other harmful incidents:
Explain what happened naturally.
Organize their story into structured incident information.
Preserve relevant evidence and identifiers.
Connect their incident with potentially related incidents.
Discover patterns across otherwise fragmented reports.
Explain why two incidents may be connected.
Help the user understand appropriate next steps.
Help prepare information needed for official reporting.
The core technological differentiator is:
AI agents acting on connected graph data using FalkorDB.
Project Yama is NOT:
a vigilante platform,
a doxxing platform,
a public accusation database,
an AI judge,
an AI police officer,
or a system that declares someone guilty.
The system deals with:
reports, allegations, evidence, entities, relationships, and patterns.
It must always distinguish those concepts from established guilt.

2. CORE PRODUCT PRINCIPLE
The fundamental user journey is:
Narrate → Confirm → Connect → Understand → Act

4. FIRST-RUN UX
The first experience should be extremely simple.
Do NOT confront the user with a complicated form.
The opening screen should reassure the user without making unrealistic
promises.
Never say:
"Criminals never win."
Instead communicate ideas such as:
"You do not need to figure everything out right now."
"You can start by telling us what happened."
"We'll help organize the information step by step."
"Preserving evidence and connecting related reports can make action easier."
The interface may show a small number of verified statistics concerning
reporting, crime resolution, recovery, investigation, or justice.
However:
statistics must come from authoritative sources;
their jurisdiction and date must be visible;
outdated statistics must not be presented as current;
statistics must never guarantee an outcome;
statistics should encourage action rather than create fear.
Never fabricate a statistic for emotional reassurance.

5. THREE Yama AGENTS
The main interface presents three visually distinctive,
dithered-style AI avatars.
Each agent has a specific responsibility.
Agent 1 — Saathi
ROLE:
Incident Intake Agent
PERSONALITY:
Warm, patient, conversational, non-judgmental.
MISSION:
Help the user explain what happened without making them feel interrogated.
Saathi:
accepts voice or text;
supports natural conversation;
supports English and Hindi;
should eventually support Hinglish/code-switching;
extracts facts silently while the user talks;
asks only contextually useful follow-up questions;
identifies missing important information;
helps organize timelines;
helps identify available evidence;
never pressures the user;
never determines guilt.
Example introduction:
"I'm Saathi. Tell me what happened in your own words.
You don't need to organize everything — I'll help with that."

Agent 2 — Drishti
ROLE:
Graph Intelligence Agent
PERSONALITY:
Calm, analytical, precise and evidence-driven.
MISSION:
Analyze FalkorDB and discover connections between the current incident
and other reports.
Drishti:
performs graph traversal;
performs entity resolution;
detects shared identifiers;
detects modus-operandi similarity;
discovers multi-hop connections;
calculates match scores;
distinguishes strong evidence from weak similarity;
explains every meaningful graph connection;
identifies contradictory evidence;
never claims that a match proves guilt.
Example introduction:
"I'm Drishti. I look for connections between incidents and explain
exactly why two reports may be related."

Agent 3 — Disha
ROLE:
Action and Resolution Agent
PERSONALITY:
Direct, procedural, reassuring and practical.
MISSION:
Turn the organized incident into useful next actions.
Disha may help with:
evidence preservation;
complaint preparation;
jurisdiction guidance;
official reporting channels;
cybercrime reporting;
police reporting guidance;
emergency resources;
generating an incident summary;
generating an evidence manifest;
creating a chronological timeline;
explaining what information authorities may require.
Disha does NOT pretend to file an FIR or complaint unless a legitimate
official integration actually exists.
Example introduction:
"I'm Disha. Once your report is organized, I'll help you understand
what you can do next."

12. MATCHING ENGINE
Project Yama uses a weighted graph-based matching model.
Do NOT calculate:
"Probability that this person committed the crime."
Instead calculate at least two independent concepts:
ENTITY LINKAGE SCORE
How strongly do identifiers suggest that two reports reference the
same underlying entity?
INCIDENT PATTERN SIMILARITY
How similar are the behaviors, sequence, technique, context,
communication path and modus operandi?
These must never be represented as guilt probabilities.
Initial example weights:
Same phone number:
+40
Same UPI / bank / payment account:
+40
Same crypto wallet:
+40
Same exact social account:
+35
Same email:
+35
Same domain:
+25
Same uploaded image or strongly matching image reference:
+25
Same alias:
+15
Same unusual request:
+15
Same modus-operandi sequence:
+15
Same communication transition:
+10
Geographic overlap:
+8
Temporal similarity:
+5
Narrative semantic similarity:
+5
These are STARTING weights.
They are configurable and may later be empirically calibrated.
High-value direct identifiers should generally outweigh generic text
similarity.
The system should also support NEGATIVE evidence.
Examples:
conflicting verified identifiers;
incompatible timelines;
contradictory locations;
clearly different entities;
evidence indicating that an identifier was spoofed;
copied or brigaded reports.
Negative evidence should reduce confidence.

14. SIMILAR CASE DISCOVERY
After a report is processed, Project Yama can show potentially related
cases.
Initially, approximately 30% of the available advanced case intelligence
is available in the free result.
This should be enough for the user to understand:
that relevant connections exist;
broadly why a connection was found;
the strength category;
and what actions remain available.
The remaining advanced intelligence is monetized.
Price:
$10 USD to unlock the next 10 advanced case details.
A "detail" may include things such as:
another relevant incident connection;
another graph path;
an additional shared identifier category;
a deeper modus-operandi comparison;
another timeline correlation;
an additional evidence relationship;
a non-sensitive agent analysis insight.
Payment NEVER unlocks personal victim information.
Payment NEVER converts hidden private information into public information.
Payment NEVER unlocks addresses, private phone numbers, private victim
identities, intimate evidence, or other prohibited sensitive information.

15. SAFETY INFORMATION MUST NEVER BE PAYWALLED
Regardless of payment status, users must always receive access to:
emergency guidance;
immediate safety guidance;
evidence-preservation instructions;
relevant helpline information;
official reporting options;
complaint-preparation basics;
information already supplied by that user;
critical warnings concerning imminent danger.
Monetization applies to deeper analytical intelligence,
not essential safety assistance.
Avoid manipulative monetization such as:
"We know who attacked you. Pay $10 to see."
Instead:
"We found additional potentially related incident intelligence.
Unlock 10 additional connection insights."

18. FALSE ACCUSATION DEFENSE
Project Yama must use neutral terminology.
Prefer:
"reported subject"
"reported alias"
"person referenced in the report"
"potentially related entity"
"possible entity linkage"
"related incident"
"reported behavior"
Avoid automatically calling someone:
"criminal"
"rapist"
"terrorist"
"scammer"
"thief"
unless describing an established public legal finding where that terminology
is supported by an authoritative source.
Multiple reports are NOT automatically proof.
Five users making the same allegation means:
"Five reports contain similar allegations."
It does not automatically mean:
"The accused committed five crimes."

21. HIGH-RISK INCIDENTS
Certain categories require additional caution, particularly:
terrorism;
sexual violence;
stalking;
child safety;
trafficking;
hate crimes;
threats of imminent violence.
For these categories:
prioritize immediate safety;
avoid speculative identification;
avoid suggestive image matching;
avoid exposing another complainant;
distinguish allegations from verification;
encourage appropriate official reporting;
preserve evidence carefully.
Graph analysis should assist investigation,
not replace competent authorities.

22. SEARCH AND IMAGE MATCHING
Images may become graph evidence, but image similarity must be handled
carefully.
The system should distinguish:
"Same uploaded image"
from:
"AI believes these faces represent the same person."
The latter carries considerably greater uncertainty.
Do not tell a user:
"We found your attacker."
Prefer:
"Another report contains potentially related identifying evidence."
Especially for violent or sexual crimes, avoid interactions that could
unintentionally influence witness memory.
